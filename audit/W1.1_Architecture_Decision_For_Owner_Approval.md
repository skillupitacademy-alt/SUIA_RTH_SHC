## Exact decision proposed

1. SUIA (`skillup`) and RTH (`realtutorialhub`) own independent learner accounts,
   credentials/session boundaries and learner records. The same person can have
   two independent accounts. Matching email or bare IDs never links accounts or
   confers access. A qualified learner reference includes the verified platform
   and its local learner ID. Do not add a duplicate tenantId field by default.
2. SHC may retain platform-specific infrastructure projections keyed by the
   originating platform and local ID. A projection is a service cross-reference,
   not a shared global learner account or permission to cross platforms. Preserve
   the current observed originalUserId/local-ID and shadowUserId/projection-ID
   mapping; do not repurpose or deprecate token fields in this decision.
3. `shared` describes resource scope. Access still requires the applicable
   resource policy, verified caller, tenant entitlement and action permission.
   SHC administrators and service principals remain distinct from learners.
   A platforms array alone never authorizes a learner across both platforms.
4. Amend audit/FinalAechitecture.md and dependent execution-plan requirements to
   replace global learner account/membership assumptions with these rules. Keep
   staff/service infrastructure identity where applicable. Reconcile all affected
   summaries, not only the three most visible learner paragraphs.
5. Authorize documentation reconciliation and read-only discovery of session
   persistence and people email consumers. Investigate tenant-scoped projection
   uniqueness as a candidate correction; approval here does NOT authorize schema,
   credential/session changes, migration execution, data cleanup, token redesign,
   impersonation, SSO, cross-tenant account linking, deployment or a main merge.

## Why this is narrower than the submitted amendment

Sonnet's amendment includes controlled cross-tenant account linking and an
offline-student link record. These are not needed to grant each independent
account its own entitlement. They are excluded unless separately requested.
Its statement that different accounts mean different people should instead mean
distinct account identities; human identity is not inferred either way.

The people schema and initial migration declare unique email, which conflicts
with duplicate-email projections if deployed as written. Composite email/platform
uniqueness is a candidate, not automatically an approved migration. Email-only
consumers and staff authentication must be made unambiguous before any constraint
is relaxed. Existing source routing does not prove deployed DB endpoints.
